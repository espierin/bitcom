Bioinformatics & IT Committee

Helping EFI use data and digital technology in ways that are secure, interoperable, practical and scientifically sound.

Our mission

Digital foundations for H&I

BITCom is EFI’s central advisory body for bioinformatics, digital infrastructure and information technology. It advises the EFI Executive Committee and other EFI bodies on the digital questions that matter to Histocompatibility and Immunogenetics: data management, software systems, interoperability, information security and the adoption of new technology.

Its mission is to promote the secure, reliable and interoperable use of digital tools and data standards across EFI. This means ensuring that EFI policy, standards and operations are grounded in sound IT and data principles, while keeping pace with international best practice and technological progress.

Meet the committee
Established in 2018
BITCom began by supporting EFI’s website renewal and providing advice on IT and bioinformatics. Its remit has since grown into a broader, coordinated role across digital infrastructure, data standardisation and education.

What BITCom does

From strategy to implementation

Advise and evaluate

Provide expert recommendations on IT, bioinformatics and data-related topics, and evaluate technical options for EFI’s website, databases, communication systems and other digital services.

Make data usable across boundaries

Develop practical guidance for data representation, transfer and security; support interoperability between laboratories, registries and partner organisations; and promote data integrity, transparency and FAIR principles.

Prepare EFI for what is next

Monitor developments relevant to H&I, identify opportunities and risks, and help EFI integrate valuable advances into policies, procedures, education and scientific work.

One connected EFI

Collaboration is central to the mission

BITCom works with the Standards & Quality Assurance, Accreditation, Scientific and Education Committees, as well as the EFI Executive Committee. Its contribution is to connect the technical, laboratory and scientific perspectives so that an idea can be implemented coherently—not merely discussed in isolation.

In practical terms, that can mean helping shape interoperable data requirements, supporting digital learning, reviewing the technical implications of a new process, or advising how EFI systems should evolve to remain dependable and useful for members.

What success looks like
EFI activities are digitally robust, future-oriented and scientifically sound; data remain meaningful when exchanged; and members can use reliable tools and resources without unnecessary technical barriers.
