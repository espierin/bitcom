Continue with saved results
?How do I use saved results?
  1. Select your case and the exact input type. Case 02 has independent genomic and CDS inputs; Case 05 has separate protein and CDS records.
  2. Paste your protein FASTA or choose your protein file. Click Verify sequence. Every residue must match the stored prediction, including any unknown amino acid. Whitespace and the FASTA name do not affect matching.
  3. Enter the ungapped position in your selected input, counted from its first amino acid. Check that its amino-acid letter matches your alignment. A removed signal peptide or an insertion can change this position.
  4. Optionally enter the corresponding position in the reference protein, based on your alignment. Equal position numbers do not always mean equivalent residues. For another reference allele, these reference predictions do not apply.
  5. Read the amino acid, RSA and graph. Write your interpretation in the workbook before opening the explanation.
!Prediction limits and numbering

NetSurfP predicts properties from a supplied amino-acid sequence. It does not confirm expression, folding, splicing or immune recognition. A short or hypothetical translation is not proof that such a protein exists. Genomic splice-site changes may not define a unique protein sequence; do not invent one to obtain a prediction.

RSA is relative solvent accessibility (0 to 1). ASA is absolute accessible surface area (Å²). The workshop uses 0.25 as an indicative exposure threshold. Retain the numeric value and uncertainty, particularly near the threshold. Map precursor numbering to mature/PDB numbering using your actual sequence and structure, rather than subtracting a universal offset.

The original manual names NetSurfP-2.0. This workshop uses NetSurfP-3.0 for both live submissions and the saved dataset. Values from different versions need not be identical.

Working without internet

Download the offline package (ZIP). Extract all files to a folder, then open index.html from that folder. The saved alignments, NetSurfP viewer and data files work locally.

Use a saved prediction in four steps

  1. Choose the case and input you analysed. Case 02 genomic and Case 02 CDS are different inputs. For Case 05, choose the supplied protein or the translation of the CDS according to your earlier work.
  2. Open your protein from Step 2. Paste its amino-acid sequence or use the file picker. Click Verify sequence. The viewer checks every residue against the archived input. If it reports a mismatch, recheck the case, translation and whether the leader peptide is present. Keep your own sequence intact.
  3. Enter a position from your variant list. Count from the first amino acid in the verified protein, starting at 1 and ignoring alignment gaps. To add a reference value, enter its corresponding position separately. Check the equivalence in your alignment first.
  4. Click Show residue and graph. Confirm the amino-acid letter in the table. Read the RSA value and use the graph for context. Use Download this observation to keep a text copy, then add your interpretation to the surface-exposure field of your workbook.

Use the CSV if you cannot use the interactive viewer. Open the matching case CSV in a spreadsheet, find your value in the position column, check amino_acid, and read rsa. If everything opens in one column, import the file with a comma delimiter.

Keeping the results available

Inputs and complete outputs are committed to the public BITCom repository. They do not expire when a DTU job disappears. Download the ZIP before the workshop and keep a second local copy. The CSV files can be read without internet, and the included offline viewer can be opened from the extracted folder. No daily renewal or automatic resubmission is required.
