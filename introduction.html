About this workshop

You will follow an HLA sequence from genomic DNA through coding sequence and protein to a structural and immunological interpretation. The course starts with downloading and copying a sequence and builds towards a reasoned interpretation of a synthetic allele.

What you will learn

  1. Retrieve an HLA FASTA record and distinguish genomic DNA, CDS and protein.
  2. Use annotation and translation to identify exons and obtain the relevant protein sequence.
  3. Find a close reference with BLAST, inspect an alignment and document differences with consistent numbering.
  4. Investigate premature stops, frameshifts and splice-site changes using the appropriate sequence level.
  5. Compare ARD nucleotide and protein sequence and explain relative G/P-group implications.
  6. Combine accessibility predictions and a structure view to formulate a cautious immune-recognition hypothesis.
  7. Map residues onto an HLA structure and document the evidence, assumptions and remaining questions.

How the workshop is organised

The first block runs from 11:30 to 12:15 and covers Steps 1 and 2. After lunch, the second block runs from 13:15 to 14:00 and covers Steps 3 and 4. The facilitator will guide the pace and live tool submissions. Complete your assigned case before exploring additional cases.

  1. Step 1 produces your reference genomic, CDS and protein files.
  2. Step 2 produces your synthetic sequences, reference choice and protein alignment.
  3. Step 3 produces your variant interpretation and five-bullet conclusion.
  4. Step 4 adds structural mapping and a screenshot.

Prepare your workspace

  1. Create a folder named EFI-HLA-workshop on your computer.
  2. Use Notepad on Windows or TextEdit in plain-text mode on Mac for sequence files. Keep your sequence files and screenshots in your workshop folder. Download your assigned input from Downloads.
  3. Open the digital workbook to record evidence and keep rough notes; it opens in a separate window so you can keep the instructions alongside it. No Word document is needed. Save the sequence files with the names specified in the manual.
  4. Use ? when you need help with an action. Use ! for additional explanation. Use the screen icon for real, expandable examples with a separate HLA-B demonstration sequence. Use the magnifier to open the complete image in a popup on this page; keep using your assigned case and its own settings. Open case answers after attempting the analysis.
  5. If a service is unavailable, follow Group guidance and record the fallback used.

Pause and continue later

  1. Tick “I have completed task” at the end of each task. All tasks on preceding pages must be checked before the next task page opens. Reading alone does not mark tasks complete. Workbook, help and downloads remain accessible. If a check is optional, inapplicable or unresolved, record the reason in your workbook and tick it once you have dealt with it. Task completion records your work, including an honest uncertainty.
  2. Your reading position, completed tasks, workbook answers and scratchpad are saved in this browser. Return to the workshop and choose “Continue” in the saved-progress panel.
  3. Before leaving, open My workbook and choose “Download backup (.json)”. Keep this file in your workshop folder. It includes your answers, notes and progress; downloaded sequence files and screenshots remain separate files.
  4. On another device or browser, open My workbook, choose “Restore backup” and select your JSON file. Restoring replaces the current answers and notes, and any progress included in the backup.
  5. Use “Download report (.txt)” for a readable copy, or “Print / Save as PDF” for a PDF. These reports cannot be restored as a backup.

At present, saving is local: it is not connected to an email address and does not synchronise between devices. Avoid private browsing, and keep a JSON backup before clearing browser data or changing computers. People sharing the same browser profile also share its workshop record.

Check your learning

At the end, use your workbook to show a partner how you obtained the protein, chose a comparator, located a change and interpreted it. Show the evidence for your conclusion and identify one uncertainty. Download your report and backup before leaving.
