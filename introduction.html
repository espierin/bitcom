About this workshop

You will follow an HLA sequence from genomic DNA through coding sequence and protein to a structural and immunological interpretation. The course starts with downloading and copying a sequence and builds towards a reasoned interpretation of a synthetic allele.

What you will learn

  1. Retrieve an HLA FASTA record and distinguish genomic DNA, CDS and protein.
  2. Use annotation and translation to identify exons and obtain the relevant protein sequence.
  3. Find a close reference with BLAST, inspect an alignment and document differences with consistent numbering.
  4. Investigate premature stops, frameshifts and splice-site changes using the appropriate sequence level.
  5. Compare ARD nucleotide and protein sequence and explain relative G/P-group implications.
  6. Combine accessibility predictions and a structure view to formulate a cautious immune-recognition hypothesis.
  7. Map residues onto an HLA structure and document the evidence, assumptions and remaining questions.

How the workshop is organised

The first block runs from 11:30 to 12:15 and covers Steps 1 and 2. After lunch, the second block runs from 13:15 to 14:00 and covers Steps 3 and 4. The facilitator will guide the pace and live tool submissions. Complete your assigned case before exploring additional cases.

  1. Step 1 guides you through saving the reference genomic DNA, CDS and protein sequence in My workbook.
  2. Step 2 produces your synthetic sequences, reference choice and protein alignment.
  3. Step 3 produces your variant interpretation and five-bullet conclusion.
  4. Step 4 adds structural mapping and a screenshot.

Prepare your workspace

For each task, use this order. Open the named input, perform the steps in the tool, save the specified output, and record the evidence in the linked workbook field. Then tick the task at its end. If a check is optional, unavailable or unresolved for your input, record the reason before ticking.

  1. Open the assigned FASTA shown above. If you are a guest, use the case assigned by your facilitator from Downloads → Practice cases and record it in Assigned case and reference allele. Keep a folder for your workbook backup and any screenshots you export from Mol*.
  2. Open the digital workbook in its own window. Paste working sequence text, tool output and conclusions into the linked field at each task. Use Scratchpad for rough notes or separately labelled practice and independent inputs. Use each field’s Instructions links to return to the workshop.
  3. Use ? when you need help with an action. Use ! for additional explanation. Use the screen icon for real, expandable examples with a separate HLA-B demonstration sequence. Use the magnifier to open the complete image in a popup on this page. Hover, focus or tap a numbered marker to read what that control does. Use “Show where” next to an instruction for a quick view of where to click, paste or save; keep using your assigned case and its own settings. Open case answers after attempting the analysis.
  4. If a service is unavailable, follow and record the fallback used.

Pause and continue later

  1. Tick “I have completed task” at the end of each task. All tasks on preceding pages must be checked before the next task page opens. Reading alone does not mark tasks complete. Workbook, help and downloads remain accessible. If a check is optional, inapplicable or unresolved, record the reason in Uncertainty and fallback results and tick it once you have dealt with it. Task completion records your work, including an honest uncertainty.
  2. Your reading position, completed tasks, workbook answers and scratchpad are saved in this browser. Sign in by email to sync them to the server and continue on another device. Use the same address when you sign in again.
  3. Before leaving, open My workbook and choose “Download backup (.json)”. Keep this file in your workshop folder. It includes pasted sequences, answers, notes and progress. Keep any original downloads and exported screenshots alongside it.
  4. On another device or browser, open Download and restore in My workbook, choose “Restore backup” and select your JSON file. Restoring replaces the current answers and notes, and any progress included in the backup.
  5. Use “Download report (.txt)” for a readable copy, or “Print / Save as PDF” for a PDF. These reports cannot be restored as a backup.

At present, saving is local: it is not connected to an email address and does not synchronise between devices. Avoid private browsing, and keep a JSON backup before clearing browser data or changing computers. People sharing the same browser profile also share its workshop record.

Check your learning

At the end, use your workbook conclusion to show a partner how you obtained the protein, chose a comparator, located a change and interpreted it. Show the evidence for your conclusion and identify one uncertainty. Open Download and restore to save your report and backup before leaving.
