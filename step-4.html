4.1 Find a proper HLA-A*02:01 PDB model (RCSB)

Goal

Pick a structure that is usable for surface mapping. You want at least:

HLA heavy chain (HLA-A*02:01)

beta2-microglobulin

a bound peptide (preferred, because it orients the groove)

What to do

1. Open RCSB PDB.

2. Search for: HLA-A*02:01

3. Verify the allele on the candidate entry. Use A*02:01 for the manual example. For an assigned A*01 case, choose an appropriate A*01 comparator structure if available, or label A*02:01 as a homologous template.

4. Check that the structure contains beta2-microglobulin and a bound peptide.

5. Prefer X-ray or cryo-EM structures (defined coordinates).

6. Pick one PDB entry as your reference model.

Examples of PDB entries that work for HLA-A*02:01 (examples only)

7M8S (HLA-A*02:01 with peptide; useful for groove orientation)

5F9J (HLA-A*02:01 with peptide)

If you need an antibody/Fab present (optional), choose a pMHC structure that includes a Fab chain

Copy / Paste / Save

COPY: PDB ID, title (1 line), and which components are present (heavy chain / beta2m / peptide / extras).

SAVE: in PDB model and components ↗, record the PDB ID, title, reason, heavy chain, β2-microglobulin, peptide and any extra components. Add the method and resolution if available.

In My workbook: PDB ID, chain and model choice ↗.

?Help me through this step

Work through it

  1. On the RCSB summary page, note the PDB ID next to the title. Read the title and Macromolecule Content to identify the HLA heavy chain, β2-microglobulin and peptide. Check that these belong to the biological assembly you will open.
  2. Use 7M8S to follow the HLA-A*02:01 example, or choose a structure that matches your comparator. 3BO8 is an HLA-A*01:01 example. Record when you are using a different allotype as a template. Record the template and whether it differs from your comparator in PDB model and components ↗.
  3. Below the assembly picture, click Structure in Explore in 3D. The page title should read 3D View followed by your chosen PDB ID.

Check your result or recover

In PDB model and components ↗, record PDB ID, assembly, chain IDs, experimental method and your reason for choosing it. Check that the segment has coordinates. If it is missing, try an appropriate alternative or record that limitation.

!Understand what you are doing

What the result means

A PDB entry can contain multiple copies of a complex, engineered features and only part of the full precursor. A correct allele name alone does not guarantee coverage at your site.

How to interpret it

Inspect the bound peptide and accompanying chains. They matter for surface context. A homologous template can orient a region without representing the case-specific sequence.

4.2 Open the structure in Mol* and clean the view

Goal

Get a clear picture: HLA heavy chain surface plus your residues highlighted.

What to do

1. Retrieve the PDB ID from PDB model and components ↗. Open the entry on RCSB PDB and choose 3D View (Mol*).

2. Locate the Sequence panel and the Components/Controls panel.

3. Keep visible: HLA heavy chain, beta2-microglobulin, and peptide.

4. Hide: any extra chains (Fab/TCR/other) if present.

Important

Mol* works by selecting parts of the structure and then applying actions to that selection (color, transparency, representations, or making a separate component).

Copy / Paste / Save

WRITE: in PDB model and components ↗, add the viewer (Mol*) and which chains and components you kept visible.

In My workbook: Viewer and components kept visible ↗.

?Help me through this step

Work through it

  1. In the Sequence panel at the top, use Chain mode. Choose the HLA heavy-chain entity by its molecule name, then the chain belonging to the assembly you chose. Selecting an entity here changes which letters you see.
  2. At the right edge of the canvas, use Toggle Controls Panel if the right-hand panel is hidden. Open Components. In the demonstration, click the eyes beside Water and Ion once. The crossed-out eyes confirm those items are hidden. Keep the heavy chain, β2-microglobulin and peptide visible.
  3. If an extra protein is grouped under Polymer, its eye controls the group. Use the chain selection and component creation shown in 4.6 to separate the chains you want to keep before hiding the grouped representation.
  4. Drag the model to rotate it. Scroll or use the trackpad to zoom, and use Reset Zoom if the model leaves the view. Toggle Expanded Viewport gives the canvas and controls more space.

Check your result or recover

If the viewer is blank, wait once for loading, reset the camera and try the direct 3D View. If WebGL is unavailable, inspect the screen example and record which operations you could not perform. Record anything you could not do in Uncertainty and fallback results ↗.

!Understand what you are doing

What the result means

A representation is a way to draw atoms. A component specifies which atoms it draws. Changing a representation does not change the molecular coordinates.

How to interpret it

Keep the peptide visible when locating the groove. Hiding a partner can reveal an interface that is physically occupied in the original complex.

4.3 Prepare the residue positions you need to map

Goal

Have a clean list of residue numbers you must highlight.

What to do

1. Open Your variant list and numbering ↗ and identify the reference and variant positions.

2. Extract the aligned reference and variant positions, plus flanking letters.

3. Retrieve the model and heavy-chain ID from PDB model and components ↗. On its RCSB entry, choose Download Files → FASTA Sequence. For practice, use the 7S79 FASTA. Open the FASTA and paste only the selected heavy-chain record into PDB heavy-chain FASTA and mapping alignment ↗. Align that record to Comparator protein FASTA ↗ with SMS2 Pairwise Align Protein and paste the aligned rows below the original record under an “Alignment” heading. Use the aligned letters to map the positions.

Copy / Paste / Save

COPY: the relevant reference and variant positions from Your variant list and numbering ↗.

WRITE: in Residue mapping ↗, enter one row per site with variant and reference positions, mature number if established, PDB ID, chain, displayed residue ID and model amino acid.

RULE: If PDB residue numbering does not match your sequence numbering, document the mismatch (see sanity checks).

In My workbook: Sequence-to-structure mapping ↗.

?Help me through this step

Work through it

  1. In Your variant list and numbering ↗, identify the ungapped reference and variant positions. Use PDB heavy-chain FASTA and mapping alignment ↗ to locate the aligned site on the PDB chain. In Residue mapping ↗, keep a short sequence window around the reference site to verify it in Mol*.
  2. Find the corresponding chain letter and check its flanking letters in Mol*. The deposited FASTA can include residues without coordinates, so verify the displayed identifier and whether the site is present in the model. Hover the letter in the Sequence panel. Read the label at the lower right of the canvas and record the PDB ID, assembly instance if relevant, chain, displayed residue identifier and amino-acid letter. Put the observed identifier in Residue mapping ↗.
  3. For the HLA-B demonstration only, precursor position 25 maps to mature G1 and Mol* shows 7S79 | ASM-1 | A | GLY 1. Your case may have different numbering or a gap. Keep reference and variant positions in separate columns.

Check your result or recover

If the PDB residue is missing or the homologous region is ambiguous, mark it ‘unmapped’. For an insertion without a model residue, record flanking sites. Do not choose a different residue just because its number matches. Record the missing site or flanking positions in Residue mapping ↗.

!Understand what you are doing

What the result means

A mature HLA chain can omit the signal peptide, and a construct can omit other segments. PDB numbering can also use insertion codes or different author identifiers.

How to interpret it

A verified leader offset can explain the precursor-to-mature difference for an individual sequence. Gaps and construct differences require a sequence alignment for PDB mapping.

4.4 Select the changed residues in Mol*

Goal

Create a selection that contains all your variant residues.

In My workbook: Verified chain and residue IDs ↗.

?Help me through this step

Work through it

  1. Open Residue mapping ↗ to check the heavy-chain entity, chain and residue ID. Choose the entity and chain in the Sequence panel and enable Selection Mode with the cursor icon at the right of the canvas.
  2. Click one mapped residue at picking level Residue. Check that its letter turns green and the counter in the right-hand Controls panel shows one residue. Use the next tasks to add the rest of your mapped sites.
  3. For a separate practice run, use chain A of 7S79 and click G1 and M5. The screenshot shows the resulting two-residue selection. Return to the sites in your own mapping for the exercise.

Check your result or recover

If a residue is unmapped, keep that limitation in the table. You can practise the controls on a separately labelled model residue, without substituting that site in the analysis.

!Understand what you are doing

What the result means

Mol* hover highlighting is temporary. Selection Mode creates a set that can be used to colour residues or make a component.

How to interpret it

A selection is only as reliable as the mapping. The reference model may show the reference amino acid at a position that has changed in the workshop sequence.

4.4.1 Turn on Selection Mode and set Picking Level to Residue

1. Click the cursor icon to enable Selection Mode.

2. Set Picking Level to Residue (so you select whole amino acids, not single atoms).

3. Click a residue once to confirm it becomes highlighted.

CHECK: When you click a residue, it highlights in the 3D view and in the sequence panel.

In My workbook: Picking level and verified residue ↗.

?Help me through this step

Work through it

  1. Hover the cursor icon on the right edge of the canvas until you see Toggle Selection Mode. Click it. A new toolbar opens immediately below the sequence.
  2. The first control is Picking Level. Choose Residue. Click a known letter and look for green in the sequence and 1 Residue Selected in Controls.
  3. If the counter shows many residues, check whether the picking level was set to Chain. Use the Clear button next to the counter, select Residue and try again.

Check your result or recover

If the camera zooms without creating a persistent selection, check that Selection Mode is active. If a whole chain or single atom is selected, clear the test selection and set Picking Level to Residue again.

!Understand what you are doing

What the result means

Picking level specifies the unit added to a selection. An atom-level selection may colour only part of a residue, while chain-level picking includes far more than the site.

How to interpret it

The mode is separate from the representation. You can select the same residue in cartoon, sticks or another view if the site is present and visible.

4.4.2 Select residues from the Sequence panel

1. In Selection Mode, click the residue in the sequence panel at the correct position from your mapping table ↗.

2. To select a range, hold Shift while clicking along the sequence.

3. To select multiple separate residues, click each mapped letter once. Check that the earlier letters stay green and that the count matches.

GOAL: You end with one selection that contains all variant residues.

TIP: Click separate letters one at a time. Use Add/Union when adding a predefined group.

In My workbook: Residues selected in the Sequence panel ↗.

?Help me through this step

Work through it

  1. Check the heavy-chain entity and chain at the top of the panel. Hover the first mapped letter to verify its identifier, then click once in Selection Mode.
  2. For separate sites, click each other letter individually. Check that the earlier letters stay green and the counter increments. In the demonstrated layout a second click on an already selected letter removes it.
  3. For one continuous stretch, click the starting residue and Shift-click the other end. Check that every selected letter belongs in the intended range. The Add/Union Selection menu is useful when adding a predefined group to an existing selection.

Check your result or recover

If the chain displays a gap or lacks coordinates, do not treat its position as selected in 3D. Mark it unresolved. Check the final count and identifiers against Residue mapping ↗.

!Understand what you are doing

What the result means

The sequence row represents one chain instance. A molecule can have several chains or copies with the same residue number.

How to interpret it

Selecting an entire interval and selecting several individual sites answer different questions. Check the final set before applying a colour.

4.4.3 Select residues by clicking in the 3D canvas

1. Rotate the structure until the region is visible.

2. Click the residue on the 3D model.

3. Click each additional site once and check the selection count.

In My workbook: Residues verified on the 3D model ↗.

?Help me through this step

Work through it

  1. Rotate and zoom so you can distinguish the region. For a dense surface, temporarily hide that surface and inspect the cartoon or sticks underneath.
  2. With Selection Mode on and Residue selected, hover an atom or ribbon segment. Read the label at the bottom right. Click when the label agrees with your mapped chain, number and letter.
  3. Check that the same letter turns green in the Sequence panel. If you see a peptide or the wrong chain in the label, try a different angle or select by sequence as in 4.4.2.

Check your result or recover

If the canvas is too crowded or you cannot distinguish adjacent residues, use the sequence panel in 4.4.2. Document which route you used.

!Understand what you are doing

What the result means

A click on the 3D canvas acts on the visible representation under the pointer. It can pick a nearby peptide or another chain if they overlap in the view.

How to interpret it

The sequence panel provides an independent identity check. Rotate the view to inspect spatial relationships after the residue identity is confirmed.

4.5 Color your selected residues so they stay visible

Goal

Make the mutated residues stand out.

What to do

1. With your residues selected, click Apply Theme to Selection.

2. Choose a strong color and apply it.

3. Clear the temporary selection and confirm that the applied colour remains visible.

WRITE: in Surface and screenshots ↗, record whether you applied colour, which residues it marks and which Mol* action you used.

In My workbook: Colour and what it marks ↗.

?Help me through this step

Work through it

  1. Check the selection count and click the paintbrush in the selection toolbar. In Apply Theme to Selection, keep Current Selection and choose the Color action.
  2. Pick a colour that contrasts with the model and click Apply Theme. Clear the green selection with the button beside the counter. The chosen colour should remain on the representation you styled.
  3. If you add a new representation later, check its colours too. You can apply a theme to the new view or use the separate sticks component in 4.7. Record what your colour marks. Keep the colour key in Surface and screenshots ↗.

Check your result or recover

If the entire heavy chain changes colour, undo the theme or reset the component colouring and rebuild the intended selection. Include your colour key in the notes.

!Understand what you are doing

What the result means

Hover and selection highlights disappear when focus changes. A theme or coloured component is a saved display state while that view remains open.

How to interpret it

Use a distinct representation or labels in addition to colour. The screenshot should identify the marked sites even if two colours are hard to distinguish.

4.6 Show the HLA heavy chain as a surface (exposed vs buried)

Goal

Assess where the mapped site lies on the reference model and what that suggests about accessibility.

What to do

1. In the Sequence panel choose your HLA heavy chain. Set Picking Level to Chain and click one of its letters. Confirm 1 Chain Selected.

2. Click the cube in the selection toolbar. With Current Selection, choose Molecular Surface and click Create Component.

3. Clear the selection and return Picking Level to Residue. Keep the peptide and β2-microglobulin visible. Toggle the surface off briefly if you need to see your marked residues underneath.

DECIDE for each mapped site whether it is exposed, buried or unclear in this model. Check whether peptide or another chain covers it. Record absent coordinates as unmapped. Enter the model observation in Surface and screenshots ↗.

SAVE: in Surface and screenshots ↗, record each site’s position, residue change, surface call and a one-line reason.

In My workbook: Per-variant surface observations ↗.

?Help me through this step

Work through it

  1. If you already have a component containing only the HLA heavy chain, open that row’s Actions menu and choose Add Representation → Molecular Surface.
  2. For a grouped Polymer component, clear the current selection. Choose your heavy-chain sequence and temporarily set Picking Level to Chain. Click a residue in that sequence and confirm the whole chosen chain is highlighted.
  3. Click the cube Create Component of Selection with Representation in the selection toolbar. Keep Current Selection. Open the Representation menu, choose Molecular Surface, and click Create Component. Allow the surface to render. Clear the selection and switch Picking Level back to Residue.
  4. Inspect the site from more than one angle. Toggle the surface or use the optional sticks component to find the side chain underneath it. Keep the peptide and β2-microglobulin visible for context. Record a reference-model observation and compare it with your NetSurfP result. Record the comparison in Surface and screenshots ↗.

Check your result or recover

In Surface and screenshots ↗, record whether each site is visible on the reference surface, buried or unclear, with PDB ID, chain and reason. Compare with RSA and exposure ↗ and explain any difference. For absent sites, mark them unmapped in Residue mapping ↗.

!Understand what you are doing

What the result means

A surface around the heavy chain alone can include surfaces covered by another molecule in the complex. Use the peptide and beta2-microglobulin to recognise these interfaces.

How to interpret it

A transparent surface can reveal a buried stick. Seeing a stick through it is not evidence of accessibility. Missing coordinates cannot be interpreted as burial.

4.7 Optional: make a separate component for the variant residues (clean pictures)

Goal

Surface plus colored sticks is the clearest way to present results.

What to do

1. Select the variant residues.

2. Use Create Component of Selection with Representation.

3. Keep Current Selection, choose Ball & Stick and click Create Component.

4. Use the eye icon to show/hide the component. If you skip this optional exercise, note that in Surface and screenshots ↗ and tick completion.

In My workbook: Separate component or reason skipped ↗.

?Help me through this step

Work through it

  1. Clear any previous chain selection, choose picking level Residue and select your mapped sites again. Verify the count.
  2. Click the cube in the selection toolbar. Under Add Component, keep Current Selection. In Representation, choose Ball & Stick. Open Options and enter a label such as Mapped reference sites, then click Create Component.
  3. In the right-hand Components panel, find your new row. Toggle its eye to check that only the site sticks disappear and reappear. If they are hidden by the surface, adjust the surface visibility or use a second view.

Check your result or recover

This task is optional. If you skip it, record “optional component skipped” in Surface and screenshots ↗ and tick completion.

!Understand what you are doing

What the result means

A component stores a subset of the structure for independent display. It can be drawn on top of a separate heavy-chain surface.

How to interpret it

Creating a component does not mutate the structure. The sticks represent the atoms in the selected PDB chain.

4.8 Export a screenshot

What to do

1. Set the view: surface visible, residues colored, peptide visible if present.

2. Click Screenshot / State Snapshot (the camera), check the preview and choose Download to save the PNG.

SAVE: download the PNG from Mol*. Keep it alongside your workbook backup and record its filename and what it shows in Surface and screenshots ↗.

WRITE: in Surface and screenshots ↗, record the screenshot filename, PDB ID, chain, residue IDs and colour key, or explain why export was unavailable.

?Quick sanity checks (do these before you conclude)

If residue numbers do not appear in the PDB sequence panel, check for a numbering offset or trimmed construct. Record the offset in Residue mapping ↗ or document a new PDB choice in PDB model and components ↗.

Extra chains block the view: hide them using the Components panel (eye icon), or keep only the pMHC components.

Selected residues disappear after clicking elsewhere: apply the theme immediately or create a separate component from the selection.

In My workbook: Screenshot names, colour key and observations ↗ · Final conclusion ↗ · Download your report and backup ↗.

?Help me through this step

Work through it

  1. Set a view that shows the groove, the peptide and the marked sites. Clear temporary highlights and move the pointer away from the model.
  2. On the canvas toolbar, click the camera Screenshot / State Snapshot. Check the thumbnail and crop, choose a readable resolution, keep PNG as the format, then click Download in the screenshot panel.
  3. Open the downloaded PNG and check its labels and crop. Save a second close-up if it clarifies the site. Copy the identifiers from Residue mapping ↗ and record them with the colour key and image filename in Surface and screenshots ↗.

Check your result or recover

If export fails, record the reason in Surface and screenshots ↗. Update Peptide repertoire hypothesis ↗ if the structure changes your assessment, then finish Your conclusion ↗. Use Download and restore ↗ to save the report and JSON backup. Keep any PNG images with the backup.

!Understand what you are doing

What the result means

A useful screenshot shows where you looked and what the reference structure contains. The file does not encode your numbering map, so retain that table with it.

How to interpret it

The workbook stores sequence text, mapping, notes and progress. The exported PNG remains an image file. Record its name and what it shows in Surface and screenshots ↗.
