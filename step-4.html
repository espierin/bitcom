4.1 Find a proper HLA-A*02:01 PDB model (RCSB)

Goal

Pick a structure that is usable for surface mapping. You want at least:

HLA heavy chain (HLA-A*02:01)

beta2-microglobulin

a bound peptide (preferred, because it orients the groove)

What to do

1. Open RCSB PDB (browser).

2. Search for: HLA-A*02:01

3. Verify the allele on the candidate entry. Use A*02:01 for the manual example. For an assigned A*01 case, choose an appropriate A*01 comparator structure if available, or label A*02:01 as a homologous template.

4. Check that the structure contains beta2-microglobulin and a bound peptide.

5. Prefer X-ray or cryo-EM structures (defined coordinates).

6. Pick one PDB entry as your reference model.

Examples of PDB entries that work for HLA-A*02:01 (examples only)

7M8S (HLA-A*02:01 with peptide; useful for groove orientation)

5F9J (HLA-A*02:01 with peptide)

If you need an antibody/Fab present (optional), choose a pMHC structure that includes a Fab chain

Copy / Paste / Save

COPY: PDB ID, title (1 line), and which components are present (heavy chain / beta2m / peptide / extras).

SAVE: Create step4_pdb_model.txt with: Chosen PDB, Reason, Notes (extras present yes/no; method/resolution optional).

?Help me through this step

Work through it

  1. Search RCSB PDB for HLA-A*02:01 as in the manual or open the example 7M8S. On the entry page check Macromolecule Content and Biological Assembly. Identify the HLA heavy chain, beta2-microglobulin and bound peptide by molecule name.
  2. For the assigned HLA-A*01 case, prefer a structure of its actual comparator when available. 3BO8 is an HLA-A*01:01 example. If you use A*02:01 as the manual’s teaching template, align and label it as a different allotype.

Check your result or recover

Save PDB ID, assembly, chain IDs, experimental method and reason for choosing it. Check that the relevant segment has coordinates. If the site is missing, try a suitable alternative or keep it unresolved.

!Understand what you are doing

What the result means

A PDB entry can contain multiple copies of a complex, engineered features and only part of the full precursor. A correct allele name alone does not guarantee coverage at your site.

How to interpret it

Inspect the bound peptide and accompanying chains. They matter for surface context. A homologous template can orient a region without representing the case-specific sequence.

4.2 Open the structure in Mol* and clean the view

Goal

Get a clear picture: HLA heavy chain surface plus your residues highlighted.

What to do

1. On the RCSB PDB page, open the 3D View (Mol*).

2. Locate the Sequence panel and the Components/Controls panel.

3. Keep visible: HLA heavy chain, beta2-microglobulin, and peptide.

4. Hide: any extra chains (Fab/TCR/other) if present.

Important

Mol* works by selecting parts of the structure and then applying actions to that selection (color, transparency, representations, or making a separate component).

Copy / Paste / Save

WRITE: In step4_structure_mapping.txt add: PDB used, Viewer (Mol*), and which objects you kept visible.

?Help me through this step

Work through it

  1. From the chosen entry open the 3D viewer for the desired assembly. In the Controls panel expand Components. Use the sequence-chain selector and the entry’s component names to identify the HLA chain, beta2-microglobulin and peptide.
  2. Rotate the structure by dragging. Use the wheel or trackpad to zoom. If components combine several chains, select the chain of interest and make a separate component before changing visibility. Hide extras only for clarity and record which were hidden.

Check your result or recover

If the viewer is blank, wait once for loading, reset the camera and try the direct 3D View. If WebGL is unavailable, inspect the screen example and record which operations you could not perform.

!Understand what you are doing

What the result means

A representation is a way to draw atoms. A component specifies which atoms it draws. Changing a representation does not change the molecular coordinates.

How to interpret it

Keep the peptide visible when locating the groove. Hiding a partner can reveal an interface that is physically occupied in the original complex.

4.3 Prepare the residue positions you need to map

Goal

Have a clean list of residue numbers you must highlight.

What to do

1. Open step3_variant_list.txt.

2. Extract the aligned reference and variant positions, plus flanking letters.

3. Map each reference position onto the chosen PDB heavy chain by sequence alignment. Verify the chain and residue identifier in Mol* and record the corresponding variant position separately.

Copy / Paste / Save

COPY: Residue positions from step3_variant_list.txt.

WRITE: In step4_structure_mapping.txt add a line: One row per site with variant and reference positions, mature number when established, PDB ID, chain, residue ID and model amino acid.

RULE: If PDB residue numbering does not match your sequence numbering, document the mismatch (see sanity checks).

?Help me through this step

Work through it

  1. Open the Step 3 variant list and find both ungapped positions. For each site, copy a short sequence window around the reference residue. Obtain the selected PDB heavy-chain sequence from its Sequence view or entry FASTA.
  2. Align the reference sequence to the PDB chain and verify the flanking letters. In Mol*, hover the corresponding residue and read its chain and displayed residue ID. Record precursor position, mature position if known, PDB ID, chain, residue ID and model letter.

Check your result or recover

If the PDB residue is missing or the homologous region is ambiguous, mark it ‘unmapped’. For an insertion without a model residue, record flanking sites. Do not choose a different residue just because its number matches.

!Understand what you are doing

What the result means

A mature HLA chain can omit the signal peptide, and a construct can omit other segments. PDB numbering can also use insertion codes or different author identifiers.

How to interpret it

A verified leader offset can explain the precursor-to-mature difference for an individual sequence. Gaps and construct differences require a sequence alignment for PDB mapping.

4.4 Select the changed residues in Mol*

Goal

Create a selection that contains all your variant residues.

?Help me through this step

Work through it

  1. Keep the position-to-chain mapping from 4.3 visible. In the Mol* sequence selector choose the HLA heavy chain and the desired copy of the complex. Enter Selection Mode before collecting residues.
  2. Select one mapped residue as a test, then build your set using the sequence panel or 3D canvas in the next tasks. Check the highlighted letters, chain and numbers against your table.

Check your result or recover

If a residue is unmapped, keep that limitation in the table. You can practise the controls on a separately labelled model residue, without substituting that site in the analysis.

!Understand what you are doing

What the result means

Mol* hover highlighting is temporary. Selection Mode creates a set that can be used to colour residues or make a component.

How to interpret it

A selection is only as reliable as the mapping. The reference model may show the reference amino acid at a position that has changed in the workshop sequence.

4.4.1 Turn on Selection Mode and set Picking Level to Residue

1. Click the cursor icon to enable Selection Mode.

2. Set Picking Level to Residue (so you select whole amino acids, not single atoms).

3. Click a residue once to confirm it becomes highlighted.

CHECK: When you click a residue, it highlights in the 3D view and in the sequence panel.

?Help me through this step

Work through it

  1. Find the cursor or Selection Mode control on the 3D canvas toolbar. Click it, then locate the picking level control in the selection toolbar. Choose Residue.
  2. Test with a known mapped site. Hover to verify the chain and ID, then click. Check that the full residue is selected and that its position is highlighted in the sequence panel.

Check your result or recover

If the camera zooms without creating a persistent selection, check that Selection Mode is active. If a whole chain or single atom is selected, clear the test selection and set Picking Level to Residue again.

!Understand what you are doing

What the result means

Picking level specifies the unit added to a selection. An atom-level selection may colour only part of a residue, while chain-level picking includes far more than the site.

How to interpret it

The mode is separate from the representation. You can select the same residue in cartoon, sticks or another view if the site is present and visible.

4.4.2 Select residues from the Sequence panel

1. In Selection Mode, click the residue in the sequence panel at the correct position.

2. To select a range, hold Shift while clicking along the sequence.

3. To select multiple separate residues, use Add/Union selection and click each residue one by one until all are included.

GOAL: You end with one selection that contains all variant residues.

TIP: Use Add/Union selection to accumulate non-adjacent residues.

?Help me through this step

Work through it

  1. Choose the correct chain and complex instance in the Sequence panel. Use the mapped PDB ID and letter to locate the first residue. Hover to verify the label before clicking in Selection Mode.
  2. Add the other sites one at a time. For a contiguous stretch, try the range gesture in the manual and check the endpoints. For isolated residues use the add/union selection operation in the active viewer and confirm the previous sites remain selected.

Check your result or recover

If the chain displays a gap or lacks coordinates, do not treat its position as selected in 3D. Mark it unresolved. Check the final count and identifiers against your mapping table.

!Understand what you are doing

What the result means

The sequence row represents one chain instance. A molecule can have several chains or copies with the same residue number.

How to interpret it

Selecting an entire interval and selecting several individual sites answer different questions. Check the final set before applying a colour.

4.4.3 Select residues by clicking in the 3D canvas

1. Rotate the structure until the region is visible.

2. Click the residue on the 3D model.

3. Use Add/Union selection for multiple residues (same as above).

?Help me through this step

Work through it

  1. Rotate and zoom until the neighbourhood is visible. If a surface obscures the site, temporarily hide that representation or use a cartoon or sticks view. Hover and read the chain, residue ID and letter before clicking.
  2. Select at residue level and verify the same position in the sequence panel. Add other mapped sites one at a time and verify that the full intended selection is still present.

Check your result or recover

If the canvas is too crowded or you cannot distinguish adjacent residues, use the sequence panel in 4.4.2. Document which route you used.

!Understand what you are doing

What the result means

A click on the 3D canvas acts on the visible representation under the pointer. It can pick a nearby peptide or another chain if they overlap in the view.

How to interpret it

The sequence panel provides an independent identity check. Rotate the view to inspect spatial relationships after the residue identity is confirmed.

4.5 Color your selected residues so they stay visible

Goal

Make the mutated residues stand out.

What to do

1. With your residues selected, click Apply Theme to Selection.

2. Choose a strong color and apply it.

3. Confirm that the selection remains visible after you click elsewhere.

WRITE: In step4_structure_mapping.txt add: Color applied to variants: yes/no; How: Apply Theme to Selection (Mol*).

?Help me through this step

Work through it

  1. Keep your verified residue set selected. Open Apply Theme to Selection in the selection toolbar, choose a contrasting colour and apply the theme. Rotate the model and clear the temporary selection to check that the applied colour persists.
  2. If a theme is unavailable in this layout, create a component from the selection and set its representation colour in Components. In the workbook record the operation and the exact PDB chain and sites coloured.

Check your result or recover

If the entire heavy chain changes colour, undo the theme or reset the component colouring and rebuild the intended selection. Include your colour key in the notes.

!Understand what you are doing

What the result means

Hover and selection highlights disappear when focus changes. A theme or coloured component is a saved display state while that view remains open.

How to interpret it

Use a distinct representation or labels in addition to colour. The screenshot should identify the marked sites even if two colours are hard to distinguish.

4.6 Show the HLA heavy chain as a surface (exposed vs buried)

Goal

Assess where the mapped site lies on the reference model and what that suggests about accessibility.

What to do

1. Ensure the HLA heavy chain is visible.

2. Change the representation to include a molecular surface for the heavy chain component.

3. Optional: set the surface slightly transparent so you can still see colored sticks underneath.

DECIDE for each mapped site whether it is exposed, buried or unclear in this model. Check whether peptide or another chain covers it. Record absent coordinates as unmapped.

SAVE: In step4_structure_mapping.txt create one block per variant: Position, Change, Surface call, Notes (1 line).

?Help me through this step

Work through it

  1. In Components identify a heavy-chain-only component. Add a Molecular Surface representation to it. If the default polymer component includes multiple chains, make a separate component for the heavy chain first.
  2. Keep your variant residues visible as sticks or coloured sites. Rotate and inspect them relative to the outer surface, peptide and beta2-microglobulin. Toggle the surface or adjust transparency to see where the side chain sits.

Check your result or recover

For each site record ‘visible on reference surface’, ‘buried in reference model’ or ‘unclear’, with PDB ID, chain and reason. Compare qualitatively with NetSurfP and explain any differences. If the site is absent, record unmapped.

!Understand what you are doing

What the result means

A surface around the heavy chain alone can include surfaces covered by another molecule in the complex. Use the peptide and beta2-microglobulin to recognise these interfaces.

How to interpret it

A transparent surface can reveal a buried stick. Seeing a stick through it is not evidence of accessibility. Missing coordinates cannot be interpreted as burial.

4.7 Optional: make a separate component for the variant residues (clean pictures)

Goal

Surface plus colored sticks is the clearest way to present results.

What to do

1. Select the variant residues.

2. Use Create Component of Selection with Representation.

3. Choose a representation like sticks.

4. Use the eye icon to show/hide the component. If you skip this optional exercise, note that in the workbook and tick completion.

?Help me through this step

Work through it

  1. If you want a clearer picture, select the mapped sites and use Create Component of Selection with Representation, or Add in Components with the current selection. Choose Ball & Stick or the equivalent sticks representation.
  2. Name the component ‘Mapped reference sites’ and choose a visible colour. Toggle its eye icon. The site sticks should disappear and reappear while the heavy-chain surface and peptide remain visible.

Check your result or recover

This task is optional. If you skip it, record ‘optional component skipped’ and tick completion to indicate that you have dealt with the task.

!Understand what you are doing

What the result means

A component stores a subset of the structure for independent display. It can be drawn on top of a separate heavy-chain surface.

How to interpret it

Creating a component does not mutate the structure. The sticks represent the atoms in the selected PDB chain.

4.8 Export a screenshot

What to do

1. Set the view: surface visible, residues colored, peptide visible if present.

2. Use the camera/screenshot option and download the image.

SAVE: Recommended filenames: step4_HLA-A0201_surface_variants.png; optional: step4_HLA-A0201_zoom_variant80.png

WRITE: In step4_structure_mapping.txt add: Screenshots saved yes/no; file names.

?Quick sanity checks (do these before you conclude)

Residue numbers do not exist in the PDB sequence panel: you may have a numbering offset or a trimmed construct. Pick a different PDB entry or document the offset in step4_pdb_model.txt.

Extra chains block the view: hide them using the Components panel (eye icon), or keep only the pMHC components.

Selected residues disappear after clicking elsewhere: apply the theme immediately or create a separate component from the selection.

?Help me through this step

Work through it

  1. Frame a view showing the groove, peptide and marked sites. In Mol* open the camera or screenshot control, inspect the crop and download the PNG. If you want a closer view, save a second image instead of replacing the overview.
  2. Open the file you downloaded to confirm it is readable. Save it in your workshop folder. Record PDB ID, chain, sites, colour key and file names in the workbook. Include the allotype and call a reference template a template.

Check your result or recover

If export fails, record the reason and keep the mapping notes. Finish the workbook conclusion, download the readable report and save a JSON backup. Keep image and sequence files next to the backup.

!Understand what you are doing

What the result means

A useful screenshot shows where you looked and what the reference structure contains. The file does not encode your numbering map, so retain that table with it.

How to interpret it

The PNG is a presentation record. The editable workbook backup stores your notes and progress, while the image remains a separate file.
